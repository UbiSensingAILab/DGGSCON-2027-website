Overview

DGGSCon 2027 is the first international conference dedicated to Discrete Global Grid Systems (DGGS). The Open Geospatial Consortium (OGC) defines a DGGS as a spatial reference system that uses a hierarchical tessellation of cells to partition and address the globe. When observations, model outputs and statistics are assigned to a common set of cells at multiple resolutions, heterogeneous geospatial data can be integrated and analysed within one spatial framework, from local to global scales.

DGGS have progressed from research frameworks to international standards and open-source software, and they attract growing interest across academia, industry and the geospatial artificial intelligence (GeoAI) community. DGGSCon 2027 is a timely opportunity to bring this community together.

The conference welcomes researchers, students, software and standards developers, and practitioners in the public and private sectors who work on the theory, implementation and application of DGGS. We invite original submissions that are not published or under review elsewhere. Accepted papers are published in peer-reviewed proceedings.

Topics of interest

Topics include, but are not limited to, the following.

Foundations of DGGS

  • Design and evaluation of DGGS
  • Cell indexing and spatial operations
  • Quantization and uncertainty characterization of geospatial data
  • Three-dimensional and voxel-based DGGS
  • Spatiotemporal DGGS

Tools, standards & interoperability

  • DGGS software, libraries and platforms
  • DGGS data storage and management
  • Scalable and high-performance computing
  • Standards and interoperability
  • Benchmarking and performance evaluation
  • Visualization of DGGS data

Analysis & applications

  • Multi-source data integration and fusion
  • Multi-resolution spatial analysis
  • Data quality and provenance
  • Digital twins, simulation and modelling
  • Operational deployments and production systems
  • Applications in Earth observation, environment and society

DGGS & GeoAI

  • Machine learning and deep learning with DGGS
  • Geospatial foundation models
  • Large language models and AI agents
  • Spatial reasoning and knowledge graphs
  • Trustworthy and explainable GeoAI

Workshops and tutorials

The workshop and tutorial day is 25 October 2027, the day before the main conference. Half-day and full-day sessions are both welcome, on DGGS software, standards, methods or applications.

A proposal is at most 2 pages and gives:

  • Title, organizers, affiliations and contact details.
  • Whether it is a workshop or a tutorial, and half day or full day.
  • Topic, intended audience, and the background assumed of participants.
  • An outline of the programme.
  • Expected number of participants, and any room, equipment or software needs.

Send proposals to the contact below by 30 April 2027. Later proposals are considered until the rooms are filled.

Review process

  • Full and short papers receive single-blind peer review. Industry abstracts are reviewed by the programme committee.
  • Submissions are desk-rejected without review if they exceed the page limit for their type or fall outside the scope of the conference. Full and short papers are also desk-rejected if they do not use the conference template.

Proceedings

  • Accepted full and short papers are published in the DGGSCon 2027 proceedings, an open access collection hosted on Zenodo.
  • Each paper receives its own DOI.
  • Papers are published under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (CC BY 4.0) licence. Authors keep their copyright.
  • Publication requires at least one author registered for the conference, and a camera-ready paper submitted by the deadline above.
  • Industry abstracts are presented at the conference and are not published in the proceedings.

How to submit

  • Submission system. Microsoft CMT, at cmt3.research.microsoft.com/DGGSCON2027. A Microsoft CMT account is required to submit. It is free, and you can create one at cmt3.research.microsoft.com before the deadline.
  • File format. PDF for every submission type. Full and short papers use the Springer Lecture Notes in Computer Science (LNCS) author template. Do not change the margins, fonts or spacing. Industry abstracts have no required template: use any readable layout within the 2-page limit.
    • LaTeX. Download the LaTeX template (ZIP, 503 KB), or open the LNCS template on Overleaf to write in the browser.
    • Word. Download the Word template (ZIP, 541 KB). The templates inside are macro-enabled (.docm), so Word will show a security prompt when you open one.
    • Author instructions. Springer's author instructions for computer science proceedings (PDF, 305 KB).
